## Capability outcome

The learner can test strategic options across multiple futures, distinguish robust from contingent actions, backcast from a desired outcome, and define a monitored action portfolio.

## Observable learning outcomes

1. Convert scenario implications into strategic options without jumping directly to a favored answer.
2. Define transparent evaluation criteria, thresholds, and decision rights.
3. Wind-tunnel each option across all scenarios and document failure modes.
4. Classify actions as no-regret, option-preserving, contingent, hedging, or stop.
5. Backcast from a desired state to milestones, dependencies, owners, evidence, and trigger dates.
6. Build a roadmap with signposts, trigger conditions, review cadence, and kill criteria.
7. Write a decision brief that distinguishes recommendation, evidence, assumptions, risk, and owner decision.

## Learning sequence

| Unit | Instruction | Guided practice | Exit evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Option generation | Implication-to-option translation and option diversity | Expand one narrow recommendation into six options | Option register |
| 2. Criteria | Outcomes, constraints, thresholds, authority | Repair a biased scorecard | Criteria contract |
| 3. Wind-tunnelling | Scenario-by-option tests and failure modes | Test a sample option set | Stress-test matrix |
| 4. Portfolio | Robust, contingent, hedge, preserve, stop | Classify actions and defend trade-offs | Portfolio map |
| 5. Backcasting | Desired state, milestones, dependencies, sequencing | Build a reverse pathway | Backcast chain |
| 6. Roadmap and governance | Triggers, owners, cadence, escalation, kill criteria | Convert the pathway into a monitored roadmap | Roadmap |
| 7. Decision brief | Recommendation and owner-controlled decision | Deliver a seven-minute defence | Final brief and viva |

## Applied lab

Task: use the VT-103 scenario set to evaluate at least six strategic options and propose a robust 24-month action portfolio.

The submission must include evaluation criteria agreed before scoring, scenario-by-option findings, failure modes, reversible experiments, dependencies, resource implications, trigger conditions, action owners, review cadence, and explicit owner decisions required.

Fault injection: the highest-scoring option exceeds authority, a preferred option fails in one scenario, and a key indicator moves across its trigger threshold. The learner must block the authority breach, adapt the portfolio, and document the trigger response.

## Holdout transfer task

Given three unfamiliar scenarios and four policy options, produce a transparent stress-test matrix and a 90-day no-regret action plan in 90 minutes.

## Assessment rubric

| Dimension | Weight | Full-credit evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Option quality and breadth | 10 | Diverse, feasible options tied to scenario implications |
| Criteria and decision integrity | 15 | Criteria precede scoring and preserve owner authority |
| Cross-scenario stress test | 25 | Every option tested; failure modes and trade-offs explicit |
| Portfolio robustness | 20 | Correct classification with hedges and option preservation |
| Backcast and roadmap | 15 | Sequenced milestones, dependencies, triggers, and review cadence |
| Evidence and monitoring | 10 | Traceable support, measurable indicators, and kill criteria |
| Communication and defence | 5 | Concise brief; assumptions and owner decisions explicit |

Critical failures: exceeding delegated authority, hiding a scenario failure, changing criteria after seeing scores without disclosure, or claiming automatic approval.

## Remediation

The learner receives a new option set and altered trigger data, then repeats the failed decision stages. Prior scorecards and decisions remain in provenance.

## Evidence manifest

Required: option register, criteria contract, stress-test matrix, failure-mode log, portfolio map, backcast chain, roadmap, decision brief, viva transcript, trigger-response record, holdout output, timings, retries, self-review, and independent QA decision.
