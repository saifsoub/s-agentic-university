## Capability outcome

Given an unfamiliar policy or market domain, the learner can discover, qualify, cluster, and communicate weak signals without confusing evidence, interpretation, and speculation.

## Observable learning outcomes

1. Define a scan question, boundary, time horizon, stakeholder, and decision use.
2. Distinguish event, signal, trend, driver, uncertainty, and scenario.
3. Capture each signal with a direct source, date, locator, observation, interpretation, relevance, and confidence.
4. Reduce search bias by using a documented source-diversity plan and recording disconfirming evidence.
5. Cluster signals into preliminary change domains while preserving contrary evidence.
6. Write a decision-ready scan brief that separates verified fact, analyst inference, and open question.

## Field reference

A valid signal card contains: unique ID; concise title; observed change; direct source URL; source owner; publication date; exact locator; geography; affected stakeholder; time horizon; novelty; potential impact; corroboration status; confidence; analyst interpretation; contrary evidence; and next watch indicator.

Confidence is not importance. A low-confidence signal may deserve monitoring when impact could be high. The learner must never convert an unsupported possibility into a factual claim.

## Applied lab

Task: produce a portfolio of 12 signals for a new public-sector decision question with at least six source families and three geographies.

Constraints:

- at least eight primary or official sources;
- every card has a working direct URL and exact locator;
- at least two signals challenge the learner's initial framing;
- one duplicate or recycled story must be detected and removed;
- all facts, inferences, and open questions are explicitly labeled.

Fault injection: one source becomes unavailable, two articles repeat the same originating claim, and one attractive signal has weak provenance. The learner must preserve an audit trail and adjust confidence rather than hide the issue.

## Holdout transfer task

Without reusing the lab domain, build four defensible signal cards in 45 minutes from a sealed source pack, then explain which signal deserves monitoring and which should be rejected.

## Assessment rubric

| Dimension | Weight | Full-credit evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Framing and scope | 15 | Decision-linked charter with explicit boundary and horizon |
| Provenance and traceability | 25 | Direct, working, exact, and reproducible evidence trail |
| Signal quality | 20 | Novel, relevant, non-duplicative observations with calibrated confidence |
| Source diversity and bias control | 15 | Deliberate coverage plus contrary evidence |
| Synthesis and implications | 15 | Coherent clusters without overstating certainty |
| Communication and instruction adherence | 10 | Concise brief with fact/inference/open-question separation |

Critical failures: fabricated source, invented locator, hidden contradiction, or presenting speculation as verified fact.

## Remediation

Remediation targets only failed dimensions. The learner receives a new domain and sealed source pack; the original submission remains in the evidence record.

## Evidence manifest

Required: scan charter, search log, 12 signal cards, removed-duplicate record, cluster map, final brief, holdout output, timestamps, retries, source-access exceptions, self-review, and independent QA decision.
