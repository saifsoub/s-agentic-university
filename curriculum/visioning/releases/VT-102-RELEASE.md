## Scholar note

A driver is a force shaping the system over time; it is not simply a renamed signal. Driver construction consolidates multiple observations while retaining lineage to each source. A system map makes actors, causal relationships, feedback, and delays visible. Predetermined elements are already in motion with comparatively low uncertainty; critical uncertainties combine high impact, genuine uncertainty, decision relevance, and enough independence to create distinct scenario logics. Correlated drivers should not be chosen as if independent. Every driver includes assumptions and observable indicators that can strengthen or weaken it.

Retrieval checks:

1. Explain why two high-impact drivers may form weak scenario axes.
2. Give an example of a delay changing the interpretation of a causal link.
3. Distinguish predetermined change from critical uncertainty.

## Decision case

A steering group prefers two technology drivers because they support an existing program. The evidence shows that both drivers depend on the same regulatory decision, while a less popular demographic driver is more independent and better sourced. The learner must present a driver architecture, expose the hidden preference, and recommend uncertainties suitable for scenario construction without claiming authority to choose the policy outcome.

## Exam and rubric

The sealed exam provides 10 signals and a focal decision. In 75 minutes, build six drivers, a compact system map, an assumption ledger, and two independent critical uncertainties. Scoring: lineage 20; systems reasoning 25; assumption discipline 15; uncertainty selection 20; indicators 10; communication 10. Fabricated causal support or removal of contrary evidence is a critical failure.

## Remediation pack

For lineage failure, reconstruct driver-to-signal links from a new source pack. For systems failure, repair a deliberately flawed map and explain each change. For uncertainty failure, rescore a new driver set and test independence. Reassessment artifacts are new versions, not overwrites.
