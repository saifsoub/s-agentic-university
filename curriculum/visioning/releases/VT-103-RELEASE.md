## Scholar note

Scenarios are structured explorations of plausible futures, not forecasts. A strong set is relevant to the focal decision, divergent enough to expose different strategic conditions, plausible in causal terms, and internally consistent. Scenario narratives connect drivers, actors, decisions, and milestones; they do not merely describe moods. Cross-impact review finds contradictions. Implication analysis moves from immediate effects to second- and third-order consequences for named stakeholders. Signposts are observable changes that indicate movement toward or away from a scenario; they require sources and review cadence.

Retrieval checks:

1. Explain why best/base/worst cases often produce weak strategic learning.
2. Identify the contradiction in a short supplied narrative.
3. Distinguish a scenario implication from a recommendation.

## Decision case

A leadership team asks for one most likely future. The approved driver system contains deep uncertainty that cannot support a single forecast. The learner must choose a four-scenario method, explain why probabilities are not justified, and produce a set that reveals different policy pressures while remaining useful to the owner. One stakeholder strongly favors an optimistic narrative; the learner must preserve divergent alternatives.

## Exam and rubric

The sealed exam provides a new driver matrix. In 90 minutes, outline four distinct logics, write one 500-word narrative, run a consistency check, and derive a three-order implication cascade. Scoring: method and logic 15; plausibility and consistency 25; divergence 15; implications 20; signposts and evidence 15; communication 10. Invented probabilities or hidden contradictions are critical failures.

## Remediation pack

For logic failure, rebuild a set from a different architecture. For consistency failure, red-team two new narratives and document repairs. For implication failure, create stakeholder-specific cascades from an unseen scenario. Previous drafts remain preserved for comparison.
