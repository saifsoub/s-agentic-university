## Scholar note

Horizon scanning is a systematic evidence-gathering practice for identifying emerging change, not a prediction exercise. The working unit is a signal card: an observed change with traceable provenance and a clearly separated analyst interpretation. A story repeated across many outlets may still be a single-source claim, so corroboration must trace back to the origin. Signal strength, potential impact, novelty, and confidence are separate judgments. Analysts reduce confirmation bias by searching across source families, logging exclusions, preserving contrary evidence, and specifying what future observation would strengthen or weaken the interpretation.

Retrieval checks:

1. Explain why three articles can still equal one source.
2. Distinguish a signal from a trend using a new example.
3. State the minimum provenance needed to reproduce a signal card.

## Decision case

The decision owner asks whether a new cross-border digital behavior warrants strategic attention. The evidence pack contains an official statistical release, two vendor reports, a legislative consultation, three news summaries derived from one press release, and one unsupported social post. The learner must select what enters the signal portfolio, assign confidence, explain exclusions, and identify the single next indicator that would most change the judgment. The correct process values traceability and decision relevance over volume or novelty.

## Lab simulation

Produce 12 qualified signals across six source families and three geographies. Injected conditions: one inaccessible source, one duplicated originating claim, one compelling but unsupported claim, and one signal that contradicts the initial scan frame. Required telemetry: queries, timestamps, URLs, locators, classification changes, rejected items, confidence changes, retries, and final selection rationale.

## Exam and rubric

The sealed exam provides 10 candidate items. In 60 minutes, submit six signal cards, reject two items with reasons, cluster the accepted signals, and write a 250-word brief. Scoring: framing 15; provenance 25; signal quality 20; bias control 15; synthesis 15; communication 10. Any fabricated source or locator is a critical failure regardless of score.

## Remediation pack

For provenance failure, rebuild four cards from a new sealed pack and include a source-origin trace. For bias-control failure, submit a search-diversity plan and two disconfirming signals. For synthesis failure, recluster a different portfolio and explain one outlier. Reassessment uses unseen evidence; the original result remains retained.

## Transfer task and viva

Transfer task: qualify four signals in an unfamiliar domain within 45 minutes, then choose one to monitor and one to reject. Viva prompts: What evidence would reverse your judgment? Where could source repetition create false confidence? Which statement in your brief is inference rather than fact? What did you exclude and why? Viva evidence includes transcript, cited cards, timing, and QA notes.
